KNOW BEFORE YOU GO

Four seasons, many possibilities

Mountain weather and road access vary greatly by altitude. The information below is general planning guidance, not a live forecast.

Spring · April to May

Lower valleys become greener and orchards bloom. Higher roads and trails may still have snow or closures. Pack for warm afternoons and cold evenings; confirm access before you travel.

Summer · June to August

The main trekking window for many high areas. Days can be busy, and weather can change quickly. Book sought-after stays and guides ahead, carry sun protection, and build in flexible travel days.

Autumn · September to October

Clearer skies and changing leaves make this a favourite for valley visits. Temperatures drop, daylight shortens, and some services close toward late autumn. Bring warm layers and check seasonal schedules.

Winter · November to March

Snow transforms the landscape but may limit roads and services. Travel only with current local advice, suitable vehicles and cold-weather equipment. Do not enter avalanche terrain without specialist training.

PACK WITH PURPOSE

A checklist for the trail and road

Use this interactive list as a reminder; your route, season and support determine what is appropriate.

Essentials

Clothing & equipment

  • Layering system: moisture-managing base, insulating mid-layer, waterproof outer shell.
  • Comfortable, broken-in footwear appropriate to the route; spare socks.
  • Warm hat and gloves even in summer at higher elevations.
  • Daypack with rain protection, snacks and enough water.
  • For camping: season-appropriate sleeping bag, mat and shelter.
  • For technical activities: specialist equipment and training arranged with qualified professionals.

KEEP YOURSELF & OTHERS SAFE

Simple mountain safety habits

Altitude & health

Ascend gradually, allow time to acclimatise and take symptoms seriously. Headache with nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue at altitude needs attention. Stop ascending and seek qualified medical advice; severe symptoms require urgent descent and emergency support.

Weather & route choices

Check local forecasts, road conditions and trail reports on the day. Tell a trusted person your route and expected return. Carry navigation, stay within your ability, and turn back early if visibility, weather or health deteriorates.

Responsible tourism

Pack out all rubbish, minimise single-use plastics, use established paths and never disturb wildlife. Respect local customs and private property. Ask permission before photographing people, and choose locally owned services where possible.

Plan for connection gaps

Mobile coverage and power can be unreliable. Save maps and emergency information offline, carry a charged power bank and agree on a communication plan. Do not assume a phone will be sufficient for remote journeys.
